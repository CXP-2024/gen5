Data structure

  • Three pair-local Pools per router: E/W, N/S, U/D.
  • Each Pool slot stores owner direction, VC state and buffered flits.
  • The escape VC remains in RES; only adaptive traffic may borrow Pool storage.

Cycle behavior

  • True input direction selects the pair before routing.
  • Phase parity gates RES versus Pool requests.
  • One pair-local arbiter serializes Pool read/write service and records utilization.

Safety checks

  • No Pool read conflict.
  • Allocation, migration and return conservation.
  • Owner cap respected; no credit created or lost.
  • Deadlock watchdog remains clean.

Pool Credits Move Toward Pressure; the Last Credit Stays Sticky

Recommended · pressure cap 3
Use RES first
→
Use owned Pool credit
→
Reclaim idle peer slot
  • A side may own at most 3 of 4 Pool credits.
  • A released slot goes to the side whose RES is more occupied.
  • Ties keep the previous owner: temporal locality without blind handoff.
  • An idle side returns surplus ownership, but normally retains its final credit.
Why credit ownership?

An upstream sender may inject into the Pool only when it holds a Pool credit. Reserved credits remain separate.

Why cap 3?

The hot side can borrow one extra slot while the peer always retains one Pool credit. Cap 4 is evaluated only as a full-elasticity upper bound.

Why not owner-RR?

Blind round-robin can park credits on an idle side. In tornado tests it collapsed to 0.435× throughput and 3.181× latency.

60 s — Separate bank scheduling from credit policy. The bank uses strict round-robin phases. Credit ownership is demand-aware: pressure cap 3 is the recommended policy. Explain why blind owner round-robin is a bad control and why keeping one credit sticky helps the likely next packet.

Why it works

The flows stay distributed across X rings. The private design stalls on input credits; DP-Phys lends one cold-side slot to the hot side before the output link becomes the dominant bottleneck.

Upper bound

08 / 09Evaluation

Where 1R1W Helps—and Where the Phase Tax Wins

Best case

Persistent one-direction pressure

  • X-biased 90/10: cap 3 latency −9.2%.
  • 100% +X above private saturation: 1.818× delivered throughput.
  • Long credit loop: L=8 gives borrowing time to matter.

The 100% latency ratio is not a valid below-saturation comparison.

Small win

Tornado under high load

  • L=8, eight-flit packets, inj=0.9.
  • Throughput is unchanged: ≈0.1564.
  • Cap 3 network latency: 49.37 → 46.97 cycles, −4.85%.

Sharing helps, but a downstream/output bottleneck limits the gain.

No advantage

Balanced or rapidly competing inputs

  • Uniform L=8: throughput unchanged; latency about +3.2% at inj=0.9.
  • X-opposite: paired contention gives +3% to +15% latency for L8.
  • Rapid reversal: ownership motion cannot repay phase serialization.
Interpretation: 1R1W is a specialization for asymmetric pressure—not a universal replacement for private input buffers.
60 s — Be explicit about limitations. Tornado gets a modest latency benefit, uniform is slightly worse, and x-opposite is worse because both sides compete for the same Pool. The mechanism targets persistent asymmetry, not every traffic pattern.
